Everyday sleep

Tracking young adults day by day, we found that stressful evenings led to slightly shorter sleep, and short or broken sleep led to feeling more stressed the next day.

Stress & emotions

Tracking young adults’ stress and emotions several times a day showed that stress and feelings push each other up and down over hours, and that highly stressed people get less relief from positive moods.

Le et al. (2026) Read more →

24-hour time use

Tracking young adults’ sleep and movement with wrist devices for up to two weeks showed that swapping sitting time for light or more vigorous activity on a given day predicted slightly better mood the next day.

Lab head

Joshua F. Wiley

Associate Professor, School of Psychological Sciences and Turner Institute for Brain and Mental Health, Monash University

Associate Professor Joshua Wiley leads the Behavioural Medicine Lab at Monash University and is an Honorary Research Fellow at the Peter MacCallum Cancer Centre. He completed a PhD in health psychology at the University of California, Los Angeles, followed by postdoctoral training in randomised controlled trials.

His research follows how sleep, stress and emotions unfold in daily life, then uses those findings to build accessible sleep and emotion-regulation interventions. His group pairs data from phones and wearables with modern statistics and shares new methods and tools as free software. His work has been funded by the NHMRC, MRFF and ARC, the US National Institutes of Health and Department of Defense, and the Wellcome Trust.
