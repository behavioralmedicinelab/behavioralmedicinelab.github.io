Share your lived experience

Research works better when people with lived experience help shape it: nothing about the community without the community. We are looking for people who have, or have had, cancer, and for family members and carers, to work with us.

This is different from taking part in a study. As a partner, you help us plan and run the research.

If you’re interested, use the Email us button above.

What you could do

  • Join an advisory committee for one of our studies.
  • Review videos, websites and information sheets before they reach participants.
  • Help design new studies and choose which questions matter most.
  • Help share what we find with the community.

What we offer

  • You’ll be paid for your time, and we cover reasonable expenses.
  • You don’t need any research experience. Your experience is the expertise.
  • You’ll have a named contact, a plain-language introduction and meetings that fit around your health and commitments.
  • You can step back at any time.

Study with us

We supervise Honours, Master of Clinical Psychology and PhD students in the School of Psychological Sciences at Monash University. Projects can be research-only or include supervised clinical training in sleep interventions.

What supervision looks like

  • Weekly one-on-one supervision for Masters and PhD students
  • Projects designed with you to finish on time
  • Authorship agreed upfront
  • Workload negotiated with you, not demanded

Your wellbeing comes before project outcomes. Read our ethics and values.

Current project themes

  • SleepSteps: a stepped-care trial of cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) after cancer. Projects can use the trial data, or focus on intervention development, AI chat support, qualitative work or clinical delivery. SleepSteps PhD students may be eligible for a top-up scholarship of $5,000 a year (years 1 to 4).
  • Cognition and cancer: cognitive prehabilitation for people having cancer surgery.
  • Why people sleep badly: intensive objective and subjective measurement of night waking and falling asleep.
  • Precision treatment: who benefits from CBT-I, using individual participant data from 64 trials (7,359 participants).
  • Your own idea: our daily diary, wearable and trial data can support many questions.

For some programs the School decides who supervises whom, so it isn’t up to us. You can always get in touch, and name our group as your preference when you’re asked.

PhD scholarships

International applicants. To be competitive for Monash University’s international scholarships, in our experience you need excellent grades, a degree from a highly ranked university and a first-author peer-reviewed publication.

Domestic applicants. We encourage everyone to express interest: domestic scholarships are usually less competitive.

What to send

Use the email button for your program (it fills in the subject line), and include:

  • which program and start year you’re aiming for
  • two or three sentences on which project or topic interests you, and why
  • your CV
  • your transcript (unofficial is fine)

For a PhD, please also include:

  • English language test results, if applicable (for example, IELTS)
  • any research experience (a writing sample is optional)

Please get in touch 6 to 8 weeks before the relevant deadline.

Everyone is welcome

We especially encourage applications from people historically under-represented in the health sciences. If something is making it hard to apply, contact us to talk about it.
